# Review: VMemKV evaluation-free first full draft

## 1. Executive summary

**Directionally correct?** Yes. The thesis — a standalone KVS that splits a RAM-resident T1 control plane from an mmap-backed T2 value plane, delegating value residency to the OS — is coherent, honestly scoped, and matches the planning documents. The self-annotation discipline (Japanese intent blocks, per-section overclaim guards, a 25-item "facts to verify" list) is genuinely good scientific hygiene and rare in a first draft.

**Suitable as a first full draft?** Yes, as a *design* paper skeleton. It is **not yet** trustworthy as an implementation-grounded systems paper, because several sentences describe the design as if it were the artifact, and the artifact is much thinner and — more importantly — in one respect *semantically inconsistent* with the thesis.

**Top 3 risks (in priority order):**

1. **The mapping mode contradicts the central mechanism.** The current T2 is mapped `MAP_PRIVATE` (`src/t2_flat_file/t2_flat_file.cpp:129`), while reorganization installs a `MAP_SHARED` mapping (`src/vmemkv_impl.hpp:185`). Under `MAP_PRIVATE`, every written value page becomes a private copy-on-write (effectively anonymous) page that the OS can only evict to **swap**, never back to the T2 file. That means the paper's headline claim — "delegate larger-than-memory value residency to the OS page cache" — does **not hold for written data** in the current implementation; it degrades to a swap-bound story. Neither the draft nor the planning docs recognize this consequence; they treat mapping mode as a reproducibility knob. This is the one issue that can invalidate the framing, and it must be resolved as a thesis-level decision.

2. **The paper describes mechanisms the project has decided to abandon or never built.** `TODO.md` states WAL/recovery and checkpointing are *Not Implemented*, and explicitly **deprecates `fork()`** ("Standardize on thread-safe, in-process background serialization instead of the LLD-specified `fork()`"). Yet §6.4 presents fork/COW checkpoint reload as *the* design, and the Abstract/Intro/README advertise the `mmap`/`fork`/`mincore` triad. `mincore` and `fork` appear **zero** times in code. Writing the system-completeness story around fork/mincore describes a rejected design.

3. **The entire current evaluation apparatus measures nearly the opposite of the thesis.** The macro-benchmark uses **8-byte `uint64` values** (`benchmark/bench_kv.cpp`), the RocksDB rival is **default-configured** (no memory budget, compression, WAL-sync, or BlobDB; hardcodes 8-byte values in `src/rivals/rocksdb_store.hpp`), and the `T1InlineValue` optimization inlines 1–8-byte values so the "fully optimized" config **bypasses T2/mmap entirely**. The only existing report (`docs/benchmark/20260501_t1index_vs_rocksdb.md`) scopes itself as **Tier-1-only, all-in-RAM**. Excluding results was the right call; the team must not backfill with this data.

The good news: none of these are fatal to the *idea*. They are fixable with (a) a mapping-mode decision, (b) a §6 reframing around the current roadmap, and (c) a large-value evaluation harness. Fix those and this becomes a credible design-and-characterization paper.

## 5. Related work review

**Bitcask — §10.3.** *Fair?* Yes. *Difference clear?* Yes — T1's sorted region for scan vs Bitcask's hash keydir. *Strengthen:* one axis is missing — Bitcask keeps *the whole key* in RAM; T1 keeps a 16-byte prefix + hash and verifies in T2. That's a memory-footprint argument in VMemKV's favor and is currently unstated.

**LMDB + mmap criticism (Are-You-Sure-MMAP) — §10.4.** *Fair?* Yes; incorporating the criticism up front is the right move. *Difference clear?* Yes — LMDB mmaps a B+tree (index + data); VMemKV mmaps only the value plane. *Strengthen:* this is where the `MAP_PRIVATE`/`MAP_SHARED` and dirty-writeback discussion matters most — the CIDR paper's central complaints (writeback stalls, eviction mismatch, SIGBUS, TLB) all depend on mapping mode. Tie your mapping decision to their taxonomy so the reader sees you've engaged, not just cited.

**LeanStore / vmcache — §10.5.** *This is your most dangerous comparison.* A reviewer from this camp will argue vmcache *already* uses virtual memory for buffer management and gets larger-than-memory behavior *with* DBMS control — so "delegate to OS" can read as "give up control vmcache kept cheaply." *Strengthen:* be precise that the axis is **who makes the eviction/replacement decision** (kernel LRU vs DBMS policy via VM tricks) and **whether there is any user-space frame table at all**. VMemKV has *no* buffer pool and *no* frame table; vmcache/LeanStore do. That is the honest, defensible line. Do not claim a performance advantage over them.

**FASTER — §10.6.** *Too thin, and risky.* FASTER's title is literally "…with In-Place Updates," and it pairs a hash index with a hybrid log — structurally very close to T1 + T2 + in-place update. *Strengthen (must):* FASTER's in-place update happens in the mutable in-memory region of the hybrid log under epoch-based concurrency with a defined recovery (CPR); VMemKV's in-place update is an `alloc_len`-bounded overwrite of an mmap record with **no** concurrency guarantee and **no** recovery today. Make in-place update *not* the differentiator vs FASTER; the differentiator is OS-delegated residency of a **file-backed** value plane vs FASTER's own hybrid-log management.

**Add / remove:** No new categories strictly required, but consider one sentence on **anonymous-mmap / swap-based larger-than-memory** approaches, precisely because your current `MAP_PRIVATE` path is effectively that — it pre-empts a "you're just relying on swap" critique. Remove nothing.

## 7. Concrete rewrite suggestions (targeted)

**(a) Intro L71 — stop stating design as implemented.**
Current: "…VMemKV uses T1 reorganization, T2 reorganization, checkpoint reload, and WAL replay to repair fragmentation and reconstruct state."
Replace: "…VMemKV repairs fragmentation through T1 and T2 reorganization. Its design additionally specifies checkpoint reload and WAL replay for durable recovery; in the current prototype these are design elements rather than implemented components (§7.6)."

**(b) Abstract / §4.3 — commit on mapping mode (choose the MAP_SHARED variant unless you deliberately keep swap-based).**
Add to §4.3: "The mapping mode of T2 is a first-order design decision. To let the OS page cache manage larger-than-memory value residency against the value file, T2 is mapped `MAP_SHARED`, so clean pages are evicted to and re-read from the T2 file and dirty pages are written back by the kernel. A `MAP_PRIVATE` mapping would instead make written pages private (anonymous) and evictable only to swap, which would change the larger-than-memory story from page-cache-bound to swap-bound. The current prototype maps the freshly created file `MAP_PRIVATE` and the post-reorganization file `MAP_SHARED`; unifying on `MAP_SHARED` is required for the residency claim and is part of the evaluation setup (§8)." *(If you truly intend the WAL-plus-`MAP_PRIVATE` design from `TODO.md`, then the honest thesis is different: "T2 is a volatile in-memory value materialization; durability and larger-than-memory-*on-disk* come from WAL + checkpoint, and RAM+swap bound the live footprint." Pick one; do not leave it implicit.)*

**(c) §6.4 — reframe around the current roadmap; retire fork.**
Replace the fork/COW paragraph with: "Because rebuilding T2 requires a full scan and copy, VMemKV performs reorganization and checkpointing in a background, in-process manner rather than as a foreground stop-the-world pass. A stable T1 index checkpoint is written alongside a defragmented T2 file, and the WAL is truncated up to the checkpoint boundary so that recovery replays only the tail after the checkpoint LSN. An earlier design used `fork()` to obtain a copy-on-write snapshot; we do not adopt it, to avoid fork-safety hazards in a multithreaded process and copy-on-write page-replication overhead under memory pressure. This is design intent; the current prototype implements neither checkpointing nor WAL truncation (§7.6)."

**(d) §5.4 — separate durable-design path from current path.**
Replace the WAL sentence with two labeled sentences: "*Design (durability path):* an insert appends an insert record to the WAL and fsyncs it before publishing to T1/T2. *Current prototype:* the insert appends the T2 record and publishes the T1 entry with no WAL; no operation is durable across process exit."

**(e) §7 concurrency — state the open correctness questions plainly.**
Add: "The current index publishes a rebuilt sorted region via an atomic `shared_ptr` and gates reader retries on a reorganization sequence counter. However, writers do not block on that counter, and reconciliation between a concurrent write and an in-progress reorganization relies on a single write-version re-check with a remaining time-of-check-to-time-of-use window; concurrent write/reorganize interleavings are therefore not yet proven race-free and are not tested. In-place T2 updates overwrite value bytes and the record header without excluding concurrent readers, so a concurrent reader of the same key may observe a torn value. Establishing an atomic value-read discipline (e.g., versioned records with reader validation) and a proven reorganize/write protocol is future work; the paper does not claim linearizable lock-free reads under concurrent updates."

**(f) §9.6 — say "no durability," not "checkpoint-free."**
Add a first sentence: "The current prototype provides no durability: T2 is memory-mapped without `msync`, there is no write-ahead log, and there is no checkpoint, so no state — including value bytes — survives process termination." Then keep the existing (good) list of production requirements.

**(g) §7.7 / §8 — disclaim the existing measurements.**
Add: "The repository's current benchmarks exercise 8-byte integer values with a nanobench/throughput harness and include a preliminary Tier-1-only comparison against a default-configured RocksDB with the full dataset resident in memory. These are microbenchmarks of the control plane, not the larger-than-memory value workloads of E1/E2, and are not inputs to the evaluation in this paper."

## 9. Final judgment

**Proceed, but fix implementation-grounding issues first.**

The direction, scope discipline, and self-annotation are strong enough that this is worth carrying forward — but not as-is. The blocking problems are not stylistic: (1) the `MAP_PRIVATE` mapping contradicts the paper's central residency mechanism for written data and must be resolved as a design decision, not a setup footnote; (2) §6's system-completeness story is built on a `fork`/`mincore` mechanism the project has already decided to abandon; and (3) the paper must consistently mark WAL/checkpoint/recovery as design and disclose that the only existing measurements are Tier-1-only, 8-byte, all-in-RAM against an unconfigured RocksDB. These are correctness-of-claim issues, and a systems reviewer will find every one of them by reading the repo — exactly as this audit did.

Fix the mapping-mode framing, reframe §6 around the current roadmap, and tighten the implemented-vs-designed boundary throughout, and you will have a defensible design-and-characterization paper whose (real) contributions — the pinned T1 control plane / OS-managed T2 value plane split, the reachability invariant, bounded in-place update, and an auditable responsibility comparison — are stated at exactly the strength the evidence supports. Do not add evaluation prose until the large-value harness and fairness config exist; the current results would be actively misleading if presented as the thesis.
